Dance styles

Ballet, Tap and Modern — and what each one is for

Ballet

Ages 3–18Beginner → student grade

The technical foundation for every other style. Ballet trains posture, alignment, turnout, strength and precise control, working through the NATD graded syllabus from pre-primary upwards. Pointe work is introduced for older dancers once strength and technique allow — never before it’s safe.

Benefits

  • Posture and core strength
  • Discipline and concentration
  • Grace that carries into every other style

Tap

Ages 4–18Beginner → student grade

Percussion you make with your feet. Tap develops rhythm, timing and quick, accurate footwork, and it is enormously good fun — the class where you can always hear the progress. Children who find Ballet’s stillness hard often flourish here first.

Benefits

  • Musicality and sense of rhythm
  • Speed, coordination and memory
  • Instant, audible feedback — children love it

Modern

Ages 5–18Beginner → student grade

Expressive, athletic and contemporary. Modern combines strong technique with freedom of movement, teaching dancers to travel, jump, turn and, most importantly, to interpret music with their own personality. It’s the style that most often unlocks a shy child.

Benefits

  • Flexibility, strength and stamina
  • Creative expression and stage presence
  • Confidence to perform as themselves

When we teach

Classes run all day on a Saturday during term time. We start in the morning with our littlest dancers and work up through the day, finishing with the Senior classes in the afternoon.

We also offer private lessons midweek, for dancers who would like some one-to-one time — whether that is preparing for an examination, a competition, or simply building confidence.

Get in touch and we will let you know exactly which class your child should come to and what time it runs.
