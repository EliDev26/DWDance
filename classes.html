How our classes work

Every dancer at DW Dance is placed by age and stage — not simply by school year. A child who joins us at ten with no experience will be taught alongside dancers at a similar level, in a group where they can succeed rather than struggle.

Most students take one style to begin with and add a second or third once they’ve settled. Ballet, tap and modern each develop something different, and together they build a rounded, capable dancer.

Classes run weekly during term time at our venues in Stretford, Flixton, Urmston and Davyhulme. Your first class is free and carries no obligation whatsoever.

Pre-School Dance

A first taste of dance that feels like play. Classes are built around stories, characters and music — one week they’re butterflies practising light, controlled jumps, the next they’re marching soldiers learning to keep time. The technique is real; the framing is fun.

We keep these classes short and busy, because three-year-olds have their own idea of how long an hour is. Parents are welcome to settle their child in for the first few weeks.

What your child gains

  • Coordination, balance and spatial awareness
  • Listening, turn-taking and following instructions — genuinely useful before school
  • Confidence in a group without a parent beside them
  • The first ballet positions, introduced properly and gently

Introductory ballet, with elements of tap and modern

Junior Classes

This is where dance stops being a game and starts being a skill — without losing any of the joy. Juniors work through the graded ballet, tap and modern syllabus, learning the vocabulary of each style and building the strength and precision that everything later depends on.

It’s also the stage where most children take their first NATD examination, and where our show — staged every other year — becomes the highlight of the year it falls in.

What your child gains

  • Correct technique and posture, taught before habits set in
  • Musicality and rhythm, particularly through tap
  • Their first real experience of working towards a goal and achieving it
  • Friendships outside school — often the ones that last
Styles
Ballet, tap and modern, taken singly or together
Exams
NATD primary and early grades (optional)
Uniform
School uniform required from enrolment
Ages 10–13 Grade 3 to Grade 5 60 minutes per style

Intermediate Classes

Longer classes, harder work and noticeably more sophisticated choreography. Intermediate dancers refine what they know, extend their range and start to develop something like a personal style. Ballet becomes properly demanding; tap gets fast; modern asks them to interpret rather than simply execute.

This is also the age at which children commonly wobble — secondary school, exams, competing interests. We work hard to keep dance a place they want to be, and many of our strongest dancers are the ones who came through exactly that.

What your child gains

  • Strength, flexibility and stamina at a genuinely athletic level
  • Self-discipline and the habit of practising without being told
  • Performance experience in front of a real audience
  • A steadying, confidence-building routine through the secondary-school years
Styles
Ballet, tap and modern; pointe work introduced where appropriate
Exams
NATD middle grades (optional)
Uniform
Grade-appropriate uniform and shoes
Ages 14–18 Grade 6 to student grade 60–90 minutes per style

Senior Classes

Serious training for dancers who’ve grown up with us and for older beginners who’ve found dance later. Senior classes cover advanced NATD grades and student grade levels, with the technical depth and artistry that implies.

Seniors take a leading role in the school — helping with younger classes, taking principal roles in the show, and, for those who want it, preparing for vocational school auditions, dance qualifications and teaching pathways.

What your child gains

  • Advanced technique across ballet, tap and modern
  • Recognised NATD qualifications to take forward
  • Leadership and mentoring experience — excellent on a personal statement
  • Guidance on auditions, dance training and teaching routes
Styles
Ballet (including pointe), tap, modern and repertoire
Exams
NATD higher and student grades (optional)
Extras
Audition preparation and assistant-teaching opportunities

Dance styles

Ballet, tap and modern — and what each one is for

Not sure where to start? Ballet is the usual first choice, but there’s no wrong answer — and children can try more than one.

Ballet

Ages 3–18Beginner → student grade

The technical foundation for every other style. Ballet trains posture, alignment, turnout, strength and precise control, working through the NATD graded syllabus from pre-primary upwards. Pointe work is introduced for older dancers once strength and technique allow — never before it’s safe.

Benefits

  • Posture and core strength
  • Discipline and concentration
  • Grace that carries into every other style

Tap

Ages 4–18Beginner → student grade

Percussion you make with your feet. Tap develops rhythm, timing and quick, accurate footwork, and it is enormously good fun — the class where you can always hear the progress. Children who find ballet’s stillness hard often flourish here first.

Benefits

  • Musicality and sense of rhythm
  • Speed, coordination and memory
  • Instant, audible feedback — children love it

Modern

Ages 5–18Beginner → student grade

Expressive, athletic and contemporary. Modern combines strong technique with freedom of movement, teaching dancers to travel, jump, turn and — crucially — to interpret music with their own personality. It’s the style that most often unlocks a shy child.

Benefits

  • Flexibility, strength and stamina
  • Creative expression and stage presence
  • Confidence to perform as themselves
