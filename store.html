Hair & presentation

Ballet hair goes up in a bun; tap and modern hair is tied back securely. No jewellery in class, please — small stud earrings excepted.

Name everything

Shoes, cardigans, bags. Twenty children, one changing area — a laundry marker will save you a small fortune over the years.

Buy for now, not for growth

Dance shoes must fit properly to be safe. Sizing up “to last” causes blisters and poor technique — buy to fit and replace as they grow.

Footwear

Choosing the right shoes

Shoes are the one item worth getting exactly right. A ballet shoe should fit like a glove with the toes flat and no bunching; a tap shoe needs a firm heel so the sound is clean; modern shoes (where used) should let the foot articulate fully.

If you’re unsure, bring your child’s current shoes to class and ask their teacher before you order — we’d far rather spend two minutes checking than have a dancer in the wrong size for a term.

  • Ballet: pink leather, full sole for younger dancers, split sole from Grade 2
  • Tap: black, bar-fastening for Pre-School, lace-up from Junior
  • Modern: bare feet, or black modern shoes from Intermediate
  • Pointe: only when your teacher confirms your dancer is ready — never sooner

How to measure at home

  1. Height: against a wall, no shoes, heels together.
  2. Girth: from the shoulder, down between the legs and back up — the measurement leotards are actually cut to.
  3. Chest & waist: over underwear, tape snug but not tight.
  4. Foot: stand on paper, mark heel and longest toe, measure in centimetres.

Still unsure? Ask your child’s teacher at the start of class — we do this every week.
