# 3. Copywriting

All copy is written and in place on the pages. This document explains the
strategy behind it, gives alternatives where a choice was made, and flags what
only the school can supply.

---

## 3.1 Voice

**Warm, plain, specific, quietly confident.** The register of a good teacher
talking to a parent at the studio door — not a brochure.

Rules applied throughout:

* Second person, addressed to the parent: *your child*, not *students*.
* Short sentences. One idea each. Paragraphs of two to four lines.
* Concrete over abstract. "Wobbling through their first plié" beats "developing
  foundational technique".
* Claim, then evidence. Never a superlative without something behind it.
* British English, British punctuation, real currency and phone formats.
* No exclamation marks, no "Welcome to our website", no "passionate about".

**Three phrases used as anchors,** because they do the persuasive work:

1. *"What parents tell us they notice first isn't the technique. It's the confidence."*
2. *"No child is ever entered before they're ready."*
3. *"If your child loves it, we'll sort registration, uniform and fees. If not, there's absolutely nothing to pay."*

## 3.2 Home page

| Section | Copy job | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Announcement bar | Signal "open, taking enrolments now" | Update at each intake |
| Hero H1 | Emotional promise, not a description | *"Where children find their confidence, one step at a time"* — the outcome the parent is buying |
| Hero lede | Facts: styles, ages, area, exams | Answers "is this for us?" in one read |
| Hero CTA | One primary, one lower-commitment | "Book a Free Taster Class" / "See Classes & Fees" |
| Trust row | Three credentials at a glance | NATD · small classes · biennial show |
| Accreditation strip | Answer "are they qualified and safe?" early | Positioned above the fold on desktop |
| Intro | Establish longevity and individual attention | Two specific student stories rather than adjectives |
| Why Choose Us | Four objections, four answers | Safety · qualifications · attention · performance |
| Dance Styles | Route to the right class | One paragraph each, no jargon |
| Age Groups | Self-identification | Parent finds their child's row instantly |
| NATD | Explain what accreditation *means* | Most parents don't know the NATD; the copy says why it matters |
| Performance | Emotional payoff | "Standing on a stage changes a child". The show runs every other year; watch weeks fill the years between |
| Testimonials | Social proof | **Placeholder — replace with real quotes** |
| Store | Promote the shop without a hard sell | Framed as convenience: "no guessing whether it's the right shade of pink" |
| Join in 3 steps | Remove process anxiety | Ends on "nothing to pay" |
| CTA band | The ask, with mild urgency | "Places in the most popular age groups do fill up" — only keep this if true |
| Quick contact | Tap-to-call for phone users | Three tiles, whole tile is the target |

### Headline alternatives

If a different emphasis is wanted, swap the H1 for one of these (keep the lede):

* *"Ballet, tap and modern for children who love to dance"* — plainer, more literal, better for cold search traffic.
* *"Twenty years of Trafford children, dancing"* — heritage-led; only if the number is accurate.
* *"The dance school where your child is known by name"* — leads on intimacy; strongest against larger competitor schools.

## 3.3 Classes & Pricing

The page answers, in order: *how are classes organised → which one is my child
→ what will they actually learn → when and where → what does it cost → the
thing I'm too embarrassed to ask.*

**Each age group follows the same four-part structure** so the page is
scannable and easy to extend:

1. Pills — age range, skill level, class length
2. Two paragraphs — what happens in the class, and one honest human observation
3. "What your child gains" — four benefits, expressed as outcomes for the child
4. Meta list — styles, exams, uniform

The Intermediate entry names the awkward truth (children drift at 11–13) and
answers it. Naming an objection before the parent does is the most reliable
trust-builder on a page like this.

**On pricing.** The section leads with *"No joining fee and no long contract"*
and closes with:

> *"Talking about cost is never awkward with us. If fees are a barrier, please
> do ask — we'd rather have the conversation than lose a dancer."*

That line will win enquiries. Keep it only if you mean it.

**FAQ** — eight questions, each written as a parent would type it into Google,
which also makes them eligible for FAQ rich results. Answers are 30–70 words,
never defensive.

## 3.4 Store

Framing: **the shop is a service, not a revenue push.** Every section removes a
reason to hesitate — what to buy, when to buy it, how to size it, what to do if
it's wrong — and only then sends the parent out.

Three practical cards do more for goodwill than any sales copy:

* *Name everything* — "twenty children, one changing area"
* *Buy for now, not for growth* — sizing up causes blisters and bad technique
* *Second-hand is encouraged* — for leotards and cardigans, though never shoes

The primary CTA text is **"Visit Our Online Store"** exactly as specified, used
consistently in the hero and the closing banner, with the destination domain
shown so the parent knows they're leaving the site.

## 3.5 About Us

Structure moves from *who we are* → *how we teach* → *what that qualifies your
child for* → *what they'll experience* → **safety** → *the person in charge* →
*other parents*.

The safeguarding section is the commercial heart of the page and is written to
be read by a nervous parent: six short cards, plain statements, no jargon, and
an explicit invitation to ask for the policy.

Two lines to keep:

> *"We could have grown faster. We chose instead to keep classes small enough
> that every teacher knows every child by name."*

> *"We will always say no to a request that isn't safe yet."*

**Supplied:** founded in 2016 by Bethany Donelon and Sally Wild. A 2016
founding rules out any "generations of families" or "three to eighteen" claim,
so the copy says "still with us a decade later" instead, which is true.

**To supply:** the principal's training, qualifications and one personal
sentence, plus Sally Wild's paragraph if she wants one. A short quote in the principal's own
words converts better than a list of credentials — a single line about what
every child should leave the school with is enough.

## 3.6 Contact

* H1 is the action, not the page name: **"Book a free taster class"**.
* Contact tiles come *before* the form, because a large share of parents would
  rather ring.
* Only name, email, child's age and consent are required.
* The message placeholder prompts genuinely useful detail: *"Which days suit
  you? Has your child danced before? Anything we should know to help them
  settle in?"*
* Reassurance sits directly under the button: what you'll use the details for,
  a link to the privacy policy, and "we never pass them on".
* The FAQ handles the two questions that stop a parent enquiring: *is it really
  free?* and *my child is nervous, can I stay?* The additional-needs answer is
  deliberately honest rather than a blanket promise.

## 3.7 Microcopy

| Where | Text | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Primary CTA | "Book a Free Taster Class" | Names the offer and its price in four words |
| Nav CTA | "Book a Free Trial" | Shorter, fits the header |
| Store CTA | "Visit Our Online Store" | As specified; consistent everywhere |
| Form button | "Send my enquiry" | First person — measurably outperforms "Submit" |
| Form consent | "I'm happy for DW Dance to contact me about my enquiry." | Specific and limited, not a blanket marketing opt-in |
| 404 | "That page has stepped off stage" | On-brand, then three useful links |
| Thank-you | "We'll be in touch" + when + a phone number | Sets expectations and offers a faster route |

## 3.8 What only the school can write

1. Real testimonials, with permission. Three good ones beat ten vague ones —
   ask a parent to describe the change they've seen in their child.
2. The principal's biography.
3. Sally Wild's biography, if she would like one on the site.
4. Actual venue names and addresses.
5. Confirmation of the safeguarding statements (DBS, first aid, sign-in and
   collection procedure) — these are marked editable because they must be
   accurate, not aspirational.
6. Prices, timetable and uniform lists.
